Spherical spline (``sos``)
==========================

Spline on a sphere for latitude and longitude, avoiding a planar distance interpretation of geographic coordinates.

Theory and interpretation
-------------------------

A spherical spline uses angular separation on the sphere rather than
Euclidean distance between latitude/longitude numbers. The kernel
depends on the dot product of unit position vectors; its order selects
the spherical roughness spectrum and associated null space.

.. math::

   u(\varphi,\lambda)=(\cos\varphi\cos\lambda,\cos\varphi\sin\lambda,\sin\varphi),\qquad \cos\theta=u^\mathsf Tu'.

This respects the longitude seam and shrinking longitude distances
near the poles. Inputs are latitude then longitude in degrees; the
displayed formula uses radians internally. Smoothness is isotropic on
the sphere, not on a rectangular map projection. The m branches have
different kernel/null-space definitions, so a planar cubic-curvature
interpretation does not transfer unchanged.

For coefficient coordinates, centering, null spaces and EDF, see
:doc:`/statistical_reference/smooths/theory`. The formula and runtime
contracts below specify which mathematical options NAMpy exposes.

Formula interface
-----------------

.. code-block:: python

   formula = "y ~ s(x, z, bs='sos')"

Formula constructors are parsed inside strings; they are not Python functions. ``k``, ``m``, ``by``, ``id``, ``sp`` and ``fx`` configure the term where supported. Formula ``id`` maps to runtime ``smoothing_id`` and ``fx`` maps to ``fixed``.

   * - ``by``
     - str | expression | None

   * - ``id``

   * - ``sp``
     - float | sequence[float] | None

     - mapping | sequence[mapping | None] | str | None
     - ``None``

     - ``False``

The formula compiler resolves defaults independently of runtime constructors. ``knots`` and ``select`` belong to the model, not the formula call. Runtime-only setup options are documented in the implementation API.

Identifiability, penalty and prediction
---------------------------------------

The basis penalty acts on fitted coefficient coordinates; requested rank k is not fitted EDF. Ordinary smooth centering separates the smooth from the model intercept, subject to the basis, numeric/factor by-variable and point-constraint rules. A point constraint replaces the relevant centering direction rather than fixing an arbitrary coefficient. Prediction reuses the fitted knots, constraints and penalty setup.

Out-of-range behavior is basis-specific and follows the retained fitted basis, rather than automatic expansion of its rank or knot domain. For bounded spatial domains and shape constraints, inspect the domain/constraint restrictions in the runtime notes before extrapolating.

Outputs
-------

The formula adds a term to the fitted model's additive predictor. Its coefficient block has p_term columns after constraints; this dimension differs from EDF. ``predict(type='terms')`` returns a floating array with n rows and one column per retained term for a scalar predictor. ``lpmatrix`` returns the full floating model design, shape (n, p), including the other terms. The scalar Gaussian examples below produce response predictions of shape (n,). Runtime matrix and penalty contracts are documented separately.
