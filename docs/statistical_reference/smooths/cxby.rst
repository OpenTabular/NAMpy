Convex P-spline with by variable (``cxby``)
===========================================

Convex P-spline with nonnegative curvature; the linear trend remains a separate unconstrained direction. Numeric-by variant retaining the coefficient directions required for row-wise weighted/linear-functional terms. Supply the multiplier with by=.

Theory and interpretation
-------------------------

Shape-constrained P-splines build cumulative/difference maps
into a B-spline representation. Selected coefficient directions
are mapped through a positive transform; remaining directions
stay unconstrained. In this construction, nonnegative curvature differences allow either sign of the slope.

.. math::

   f''(x)\geq0,\qquad f(x)=B(x)\Sigma c(\theta).

The displayed condition is enforced structurally over the spline
domain, rather than checked only at the observed rows. Smoothing
still controls variation within the allowed shapes. The fitted
predictor is nonlinear in the free parameters theta, so ordinary
unconstrained least-squares coefficient updates do not apply.
A shape constraint is a modeling assumption, not a conclusion
from a significance test. Derivative statements concern this term
on the additive predictor scale, holding other terms fixed.

For a numeric multiplier v the model contribution is v*f(x). A negative v reverses the direction of its derivative with respect to x; the constraint is on the underlying smooth, not every multiplied contribution.

The coefficient transformation and penalty coordinates must be evaluated together; the shape fitting route retains their derivatives. See :doc:`/user_guide/shape_constrained_gams` for centering, inference and fitting restrictions.

For coefficient coordinates, centering, null spaces and EDF, see
:doc:`/statistical_reference/smooths/theory`. The formula and runtime
contracts below specify which mathematical options NAMpy exposes.

This is a SCAM-style shape-constrained basis. See :doc:`/user_guide/shape_constrained_gams` for its inequalities and supported fitting routes.

Formula interface
-----------------

.. code-block:: python

   formula = "y ~ s(x, bs='cxby')"

Formula constructors are parsed inside strings; they are not Python functions. ``k``, ``m``, ``by``, ``id``, ``sp`` and ``fx`` configure the term where supported. Formula ``id`` maps to runtime ``smoothing_id`` and ``fx`` maps to ``fixed``.

Identifiability, penalty and prediction
---------------------------------------

The basis penalty acts on fitted coefficient coordinates; requested rank k is not fitted EDF. Ordinary smooth centering separates the smooth from the model intercept, subject to the basis, numeric/factor by-variable and point-constraint rules. A point constraint replaces the relevant centering direction rather than fixing an arbitrary coefficient. Prediction reuses the fitted knots, constraints and penalty setup.

Out-of-range behavior is basis-specific and follows the retained fitted basis, rather than automatic expansion of its rank or knot domain. For bounded spatial domains and shape constraints, inspect the domain/constraint restrictions in the runtime notes before extrapolating.

Outputs
-------

The formula adds a term to the fitted model's additive predictor. Its coefficient block has p_term columns after constraints; this dimension differs from EDF. ``predict(type='terms')`` returns a floating array with n rows and one column per retained term for a scalar predictor. ``lpmatrix`` returns the full floating model design, shape (n, p), including the other terms. The scalar Gaussian examples below produce response predictions of shape (n,). Runtime matrix and penalty contracts are documented separately.
